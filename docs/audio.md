# Sound

Homewater's sound is made in the browser with the Web Audio API. There are no sound files and no extra libraries. The code is in `src/audio/`:

- `config.ts`: every audio tuning number, grouped by what it shapes (ambience per stage, cues, heartbeat, score).
- `mood.ts`: pure decisions, unit-tested in `tests/unit/audio.test.ts`: how dangerous the moment is, which music slot plays, which ambience fits where the fish is, and which cue an event plays.
- `engine.ts`: the `AudioEngine`, which builds the sound graph and plays it.

The simulation knows nothing about sound. `src/app/App.ts` hands the engine the world each frame (`audio.update`) and the drained simulation events (`audio.onEvents`). The engine only reads the world.

## What you hear

- **Ambience, per stage:** filtered noise, a low drone and bubbles.
  - **Egg:** muffled creek water through gravel.
  - **Fry:** a lake hush.
  - **Smolt:** a rushing river at night. The sea creeps in at the river mouth.
  - **Ocean:** vast and low.
  - **Return:** coastal water, then the river. Riffles are louder and more turbulent.
  - **Spawn:** a shallow, babbling creek.

  A stage change dips the ambience and brings the new one up. The world sound passes through a low-pass filter, so everything sounds underwater.
- **Event cues:**
  - Warnings play a soft, tense sting.
  - Orca and shark warnings, and their charges, play a low pulse.
  - Bird, bear and merganser strikes and seal lunges play a splash. These fade with distance from the fish.
  - A surge of current gets a swell, eating gets bubbles, and the jack's opening gets a soft bright tone.
  - Spawning, stage completion and death each have their own cue.
- **The fish:** a soft rush that follows swimming speed, with a whoosh when a burst starts. A heartbeat comes in when energy (or fat, on the return) runs low, and it quickens as the fish weakens.
- **Score:** sparse pads, calm by default. A low, trembling danger layer rises while any predator is warning or attacking, then falls away slowly.

## Music slots

The score is organised as named slots:

| Slot | When it plays |
|---|---|
| `music.title` | Title screen and new-lineage setup |
| `music.egg`, `music.fry`, `music.smolt`, `music.ocean`, `music.return`, `music.spawn` | That stage, including its stage card |
| `music.danger` | Layered on top, with its level following the danger (0 to 1) |
| `music.end` | The end-of-life sequence and the offspring choice |

Each slot is generated by default. To replace a slot with a recording:
1. Save the file as `public/audio/<slot>.mp3`, for example `public/audio/music.ocean.mp3`.
2. Run `npm run narration:manifest`. It lists every `.mp3` in `public/audio/` in `manifest.json`, music and narration alike.

A listed slot plays the file looped instead of the generated layer, and crossfades in and out. A recorded `music.danger` loops quietly and its volume follows the danger level. Compose it so it works at any level.

Composer notes:
- Loop cleanly.
- Keep it sparse and quiet. Captions and narration sit on top, and the music ducks under narration.
- Each stage loop should be at least a minute, so the repeat isn't obvious.

## Narration ducking

`src/ui/narration.ts` reports when a recorded line starts and stops (`onSpeaking`). While a line plays, the engine lowers music and ambience to `AUDIO.duck.level`, then brings them back up when it ends. Event cues are not ducked, so warnings stay clear.

## Settings and browser rules

- **Starting:** the audio context is created on the player's first click or key press, as browsers require. Pressing Begin or Continue also resumes it.
- **No audio:** if Web Audio is missing or fails, the game stays silent and logs nothing.
- **Settings:** volume, mute and narration volume are on the **Audio** tab of Settings (title screen and pause menu). Narration plays at the sound volume × the narration volume. **M** toggles mute at any time. Both are saved in `localStorage` with the other settings. Mute also silences narration.
- **Volume by screen:** pausing fades everything down. Menus and stage cards play at a lower level.

## Cost

The sound graph is built once: about 40 nodes, three looping noise sources sharing one buffer, and a few oscillators. Continuous parameters update ten times a second, and only when they change. One-shot cues create two or three short-lived nodes each, which disconnect when they finish.
