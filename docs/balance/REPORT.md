# Homewater balance report

Generated by `npm run balance` (scripts/balance/). Seed 20261119; params.json sha256 `97e3ba1c0077`. 800 single lives per difficulty and skill, 150 spawning trials per role, and 50 lineages of up to 10 generations. 9,155 lives in all, about 333 hours of simulated play, in 1332 s on 4 workers.

How to read it:
- **Per-fish death rate:** the share of attempts at a stage that end in death. A sibling resuming at a checkpoint is a new attempt.
- **Brood:** the first fish plus its siblings (siblings param + 1). The brood fails when the last fish dies.
- **Bots:** play through the public World API with Intent inputs only, reading what is on screen. Novice, average and skilled differ in reaction time, steering wobble, missed threats, attention lapses and habits; see `scripts/balance/skills.ts`.
- **Marks:** (easy) and **(hard)** compare against the design bands in `scripts/balance/targets.ts`, which are the simulator's proposal for the owner to confirm.
- **±** is a 95% interval.

## Summary

Written by hand from the run below (seed 20261119). The bots are not people. Read each number as "a player who plays like this profile", and check anything about feel in the browser.

### The short version

- **The game is much harder than the design intends, and the difficulty sits in the wrong places.** The first two stages almost never kill anyone. Then the smolt run, the off-screen roll at the end of the ocean years, and the fat budget in the home river kill most fish.
- **Field guide.** It works for skilled players: 99% of lives completed. An average player completes 87% of lives but uses 2.3 siblings on average. A novice completes only 21%: 46% of novice broods are wiped out in the smolt run and 28% on the return. Strays carry 71% of a novice's generations.
- **Wild.** No player profile can keep a line going. Skilled players complete 12% of lives, average players 3% and novices 0%. In 50 lineages per profile, no Wild line reached generation 2 except a few skilled ones. Wild is supposed to be "closer to real odds, never true odds". At the moment it is close to true odds: about 1% of single fish survive from egg to spawning, against "well under 1%" in nature.
- **Off-screen chance is concentrated in one roll**, the extra years at sea. For skilled players it is 34% of all deaths on Field guide. On Wild it kills 64% of the skilled fish that reach the end of the ocean stage. The egg and fry stages have no off-screen loss at all, although the advisor's figures (80-95% die) are the biggest real losses.
- **Jacks are too common.** Of maturation decisions, 4-9% come out jack, against a creek base rate of 4% and 1-2% in a real run. Good feeding raises the odds, and the bots feed well. Among the males the player goes on to play upstream, 6-15% are jacks. The off-screen roll kills non-jacks, and the replacement sibling draws maturation again, so survivors skew toward jacks.
- **Stage lengths are on target.** The egg stage takes 35-37 s, the fry 16-23 s, the smolt run about 40 s, the ocean 25-28 s, the return about 60 s and spawning about 39 s. A completed life takes 4-6 minutes, including the siblings' attempts.

### Stage by stage, against the design intent

| Stage | Field guide (forgiving) | Wild (harsher, never true odds) |
|---|---|---|
| Egg and alevin | Trivial: 0% deaths for all three profiles. Fine for a forgiving mode, but floods are not felt anywhere. | Trivial: 0-1%. Too easy. |
| Fry | Trivial: 0-1%. On Field guide the trout swims slower (5.2) than a cruising fry (5.3), so it can never catch a fry that swims away. | 0-3%. Too easy. |
| Smolt run | **Too hard.** Deaths per fish: novice 88%, average 58%, skilled 35%. Seals and mergansers do most of the killing. | **Far too hard.** Novice 99%, average 94%, skilled 76%. It ends most Wild lines by itself. |
| Ocean | Visible threats are fair: orca and shark take 0-6%. The off-screen roll takes another 26-28% of fish. | The roll alone takes 57-64%. Too harsh for something the player cannot act on. |
| Return, sea and nets | Fine for average and skilled players (1-7%). Novices die in the nets on 29% of approaches. | Average and skilled players run out of fat (13-24%). Novices die in the nets (30%). |
| Return, home river | **Too hard for novice and average players** (81% and 40%), from bears and from fat running out. Fine for skilled players (8%). | **Far too hard**: 86-100%, mostly fat running out, even for skilled players. Bears force waiting, and waiting burns fat by the second. |
| Spawning | Fine: bears and "no spawn" for novices; 3% for skilled players. | A little easy for skilled players (3%), and in band otherwise. |

The per-role spawning trials look sensible. Females on Field guide spawn 73% (novice) to 99% (skilled) of the time. Large males spawn 81-98% of the time and score about 0.5× an average spawner. Jacks are the most skill-dependent role: they spawn 32% of the time as novices and 90% as skilled players, and score 0.26-0.48×. That matches the design: jacks fertilize less, but they skip the years at sea.

Trait drift over 10 generations is small: under 0.2 SD on Field guide, where offspring are picked at random and mated to random fish from the population. No trait is under strong selection yet. The larger moves on Wild come from the few lines that survived, so they are noise.

### What the bots learned (useful for captions and the owner's checklist)

- **Seals.** Following the caption's advice ("swim across its path") is the least reliable answer. Average bots that do it lose about 43% of smolt attempts to seals. Skilled bots sprint past while the seal is still a dark shape and lose 15%. A seal lunge kills anything along its path, so a lunge aimed at a school-mate next to the player kills the player too.
- **Mergansers.** The way to survive is to turn back upstream as soon as the splash appears. Swimming on into it is fatal.
- **Fat.** Upstream, bursting costs about the same fat per unit of distance as cruising, because the base drain is per second. The hint "Bursting burns it fast" is misleading in practice. Waiting below a bear, as the bear caption suggests, costs fat and often brings a second swipe: a resting bear re-arms after 3 s.
- **Fork.** Bots read the scent and choose the right branch reliably. A wrong branch costs fat and a second pass by the bear that sits just past the fork.

### Simulation issues noticed (code, not parameters; for the session that owns src/)

1. **An orphaned chase turns on the player** (`src/sim/stages/smolt.ts`, `stepChar` and `stepMergansers`). If the school-mate a char or merganser is chasing is taken by another predator, the lookup returns null and the predator chases the player instead.
2. **Poor plankton patches can spawn inside the weed shelf, below its floor** (`fry.ts` `spawnPatch`: x from -18 ± 2.5 crosses `WEED_EDGE` = -19, and y can be below the shelf floor at -6). The fry cannot reach that food. The bots skip it, but a player will circle it.
3. **Bear rest time is a hard-coded 3 s** (`return.ts` `stepBear`, `bear.timer = 3`). That breaks rule 1 ("no magic numbers"). Proposal: a new param `return.bearRest` of about 5 s, so "wait for the swipe, then cross" works.
4. **`alevin.exposureRate` and `alevin.exposureRecovery` are unused** since the light-exposure rule was removed. `AlevinView.exposure` is always 0.
5. **Brood size is one more than the param.** `siblings` counts replacements, so a brood is 6 fish on Field guide and 4 on Wild. DESIGN.md says "a brood that works as about 3–5 lives". If 5 and 3 fish were meant, set the params to 4 and 2, which would make everything above harder still.
6. **The off-screen ocean roll comes after maturation is decided.** A sibling who replaces a fish lost to the roll draws maturation again, which skews survivors toward jacks (see above).
7. **The egg and fry stages have no off-screen loss.** The advisor's survival figures for these stages (10-20% and 5-10%) appear only in captions. A flood or sibling-loss beat, as DESIGN.md plans, would carry that chance.

With the proposals:
- Field guide is forgiving for average and skilled players, and close to forgiving for novices.
- Wild separates skill strongly: skilled lines last about 4 generations on average.
- Novices still cannot survive Wild. The smolt run carries that, mainly because the novice profile swims at bird depth and reacts slowly. New players start on Field guide, so the Wild novice band in `targets.ts` (25-60%) is probably the thing to revise, not the game.
- Egg and fry barely move with parameters alone (Wild novice 6%). See issue 7.

### Caveats

- **The bots are reasonable, not optimal.** Each stage's bot was debugged until its deaths looked like player mistakes and not bot bugs, by tracing individual deaths. The skilled profile still loses fish that an expert human might save, especially in the smolt run.
- **Stuck bots are rare.** In 4 of 4,800 single lives a bot got stuck. They are counted as failures and flagged.
- **The bands in `scripts/balance/targets.ts` are the simulator's proposal.** The owner should confirm them.
